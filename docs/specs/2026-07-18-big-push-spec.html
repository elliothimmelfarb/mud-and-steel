Section 1Decision log

Fog of warThe camera leash. The commander camera cannot travel past your farthest-forward living soldier. You may look at the enemy side from where your men stand; you may not be there. No information hiding in v1 — this is a presence constraint, and it makes forward soldiers your eyes.
Match lengthVariable, chosen at match creation. Raid (~10 min) · Battle (~20 min, default) · Grand Assault (~35 min) · Attrition (untimed, strength-break only). Timed matches end at the final whistle: winner is the side holding more enemy front-line sections, tie broken by remaining battalion strength.
RostersSymmetric in v1. Both sides buy from the same roster at the same prices (German meshes on their side). National asymmetry (MG08 nests, Stosstruppen) is deferred to a later push.
HostingEverything on Vercel. Static game + serverless signaling routes + Upstash Redis for room state. The lockstep command stream rides a WebRTC DataChannel peer-to-peer — Vercel's no-long-lived-sockets rule stops being a constraint the moment the match itself needs no server. Deployed and live as of this spec.

Section 2The camera leash — exact behaviour

  • Definition. Each tick the sim computes per-side advanceZ: the position along the advance axis of your farthest-forward living soldier — trench garrison, assault squads, and marching columns all count; corpses and the possessed-and-dead do not. Exposed on SimState (it must be identical on both clients; it is sim data, not render data).
  • Clamp. The camera target (the orbit point in CameraRig.clampTarget, controls.ts:342) may not cross advanceZ − 12 m toward the enemy (sign mirrored for the north-side player). Pitch, yaw, and the full 10–170 m zoom stay free — standing on your parapet and looking across is the point.
  • Smoothing. Boundary advances follow your men within ~0.5 s; recessions (your forward men die) ease back over ~3 s so a sniper cannot yank the player's view. If the camera sits beyond a receding boundary, it glides back rather than snapping.
  • Start of match: boundary initialises to your own front-trench line (z = ±80) even before any purchase, so setup feels normal.
  • Exempt: FPS embodiment (you see what he sees), game-over flyover, replays, and spectators. The leash is a commander-view rule only.
  • Honesty note: in lockstep both clients hold full state, so this is not information security — it is exposure discipline, and it creates a real reason to push scouts and keep a toehold alive in the sunken lane. True hidden-information fog is a possible later push and is out of scope here.

Section 4All-Vercel architecture

Deployment state & signaling contract

  • Done today: Vercel project elliothimmelfarbs-projects/mud-and-steel linked (Vite auto-detected, vercel.json committed), production alias mud-and-steel.vercel.app live. Pending (Elliot, one click): connect GitHub to the Vercel account so pushes to main auto-deploy — until then, vercel deploy --prod from CI or CLI.
  • Signaling routes (Node serverless, added in M5): POST /api/rooms → creates {code} (4 letters, 15 min TTL) · POST /api/rooms/[code]/signal → append SDP offer/answer/ICE for the other side · GET /api/rooms/[code]?since=n → poll at 1 Hz until the DataChannel opens. Storage: Upstash Redis via the Vercel Marketplace integration (free tier; Elliot approves the install when M5 starts).
  • ICE: public STUN (Google + Cloudflare), no TURN in v1. If two symmetric NATs meet, the lobby says so plainly and offers retry; the all-Vercel fallback (HTTP long-poll command relay through the same routes) is specced as v1.5 if real-world failure rates warrant it.
  • Rejoin: room code re-signals within the TTL; the surviving peer streams the command log and the rejoiner fast-forwards. AI commands the absent side meanwhile.
  • Desync: 32-bit rolling hash of hot state (soldier positions/hp, req, section ownership) exchanged every 30 ticks; on mismatch both pause, the room creator's snapshot wins, both resume. Every desync is logged with tick + divergent subsystem.

Section 6Implementation by workflows

Operating doctrine for the implementing session

  • One milestone = one GitHub Flow cycle (issue → branch → PR → merge), committed in small checkpoints throughout. Every PR ends with live verification in the running game — probes driven via __game.frame() when the tab is hidden — and dev servers stopped afterward.
  • Fable designs and verifies; Opus executes the enumerable. Route stages to Opus (model:'opus') when the work is mechanical over a known list — call-site sweeps, mirroring dressing code along a parameter, config plumbing, probe scaffolds. Keep on the session model: seam design, netcode, anything touching determinism, and every adversarial-verify or judge stage.
  • Adversarial verification is not optional on determinism-touching PRs: independent skeptic agents attempt to produce hash divergence (order-of-iteration, module state, trig call sites) before merge.

Out of scope (deferred, deliberately)

  • Asymmetric national rosters — a later push, pure config once M3 lands.
  • True hidden-information fog of war (the leash is presence, not secrecy).
  • TURN servers / long-poll relay — specced as v1.5, built only if STUN failure rates demand it.
  • More than two commanders per match; ranked play; persistence beyond high scores.
