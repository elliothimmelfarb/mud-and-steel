The Big Push

MUD & STEEL — a second trench, an enemy who buys men too, and the whistle that sends them over  ·  18 July 2026
Plan of Attack

A new mode: two trench systems face each other across no-man's-land. Both commanders — human vs AI, or human vs human — spend requisition on soldiers who march in from their own rear, file up the communication trenches, and take their posts. Either side may order sections over the top to take the opposite line. This despatch covers the reconnaissance of the existing code, the design of the mode, and the architecture that makes singleplayer AI and multiplayer the same feature.

Part IReconnaissance — what the ground gives us

The sim is already multiplayer-shaped

Finding: the simulation core is a fixed-timestep 30 Hz machine (SIM_DT, config.ts:26; accumulator game.ts:1387) driven entirely by seeded, forked RNG streams (rng.ts:9–33). A grep of src/sim/ finds zero uses of Math.random, Date.now, or three — the sim is pure data with one-way FX/sound queues out to the renderer.
  • Fixed call order every tick: units → enemies → vehicles → projectiles → bullets → gas → capture (game.ts:1446). Same inputs, same seed → same battle.
  • The save system (v2, save.ts:63) already serialises nearly the whole state, including terrain deformation as a replayable op log (craterOps).
  • Three blemishes: module-global mutable state in barrage.ts:26, gas.ts:11, vehicles.ts:16 lives outside SimState — invisible to saves and fatal to running two sims in one process.

The enemy already knows how to assault a trench — we just need to lend that brain to our own men

Finding: German squads have the full attacking toolkit: wedge spawning with NCOs (makeSquad, enemies.ts:485), flow-field advance with boids cohesion (enemies.ts:341), bounding overwatch between elements (updateSquads, enemies.ts:531), shell-hole cover, wire cutting, trench melee, and section capture (updateCapture, trench.ts:88). British units, by contrast, teleport into their posts (createUnit, game.ts:994) and their only offensive move is the crude global bayonet-charge direct-seek (chargeMove, soldiers.ts:285).
  • Friend and foe are structurally split: SimState.units[].crew[] vs SimState.enemies[], with the AI brain fields (behavior, squadId, element) existing only on Enemy (types.ts:129–160).
  • Every soldier already carries a team: 'brit' | 'german' field — the seed of a faction system. flameCone is already faction-parameterised and used by both sides; it is the template.
  • The adaptive wave director (planWave, waves.ts:37) — budgets, templates, counter-purchases against your loadout — is 80% of an AI commander already.

The world is one-directional in exactly eight places

Finding: trench carving is fully polyline-parameterised (carveTrench takes any line, terrain.ts:626), and all lines carve against one shared pre-trench snapshot (terrain.ts:519) so a second system composes correctly for free. But "the enemy is north (−z)" is hard-coded as a sign flip in a small, enumerable set of places — the fire-step normal, parapet/parados side, scenery dressing, rout direction, spawn edges, breach scoring, camera clamps. The full ledger is in Part IV.
  • Rendering scales: every soldier of both armies is 14 instanced part-meshes — ~14 draw calls total whether 50 men or 500 (unitMeshes.ts:5). Hard cap CAP=560 instances (unitMeshes.ts:36) needs raising for two full armies plus their dead.
  • Walk/run/charge gaits are already procedural and driven by moveAmount — marching-in animation costs nothing new.
  • The FPS possession seam (fps.ts:256) fires the same simulated ordnance as the AI — the player can stand in either trench of this mode from day one in singleplayer.

Part IIThe unifying reframe

An AI opponent and a remote human are the same thing: a command stream

The conventional plan would build this twice — an AI opponent bolted into the sim for singleplayer, then a separate netcode layer for multiplayer. That split is a design smell, and the deterministic sim dissolves it.

Make every act of command a serialisable, tick-stamped Command — BuyUnit, OrderAssault, Recall, FireBarrage — consumed by the sim only at tick boundaries. Then:

  • Singleplayer = your commands + a local AI commander emitting commands into the same queue.
  • Multiplayer = your commands + a remote peer's commands arriving over the wire. No server-side sim needed — both clients run the same deterministic battle (lockstep).
  • Replays = seed + the command log. Free.
  • Rejoin after disconnect = replay the log at max speed. Free.
  • The soak-test harness can drive full two-sided battles headless. Free.

One architecture, four features. The AI commander is just the first network peer we ship.

Part IIIThe mode — design

1 · The ground

  • Mirror, don't invent. The German system is the British one reflected about z = 0: front at −80, support at −150, comm trenches between, rear edge at −220. buildTrenchPolylines already takes arbitrary lines; carving both systems against the shared pre-trench snapshot composes correctly today.
  • One new parameter: facing. Thread an enemyDir: ±1 sign through carveTrench (fire-step wall, parapet side) and the scenery dressing loops, replacing the four hard-coded if (nz > 0) flip tests. The fire step of the north trench faces south; its parapet sandbags face south; its duckboards nudge north.
  • The sunken lane becomes the prize. It already exists (terrain.ts:480); recut it along z ≈ 0. Holding it grants a supply-rate bonus (Part III·3) — a reason to fight in no-man's-land, not just across it.
  • Captured trenches are bad homes. A taken section's fire step faces the wrong way — occupiers get parados cover only, until an engineer "consolidates" (reverses the bench over ~40 s). This falls straight out of the terrain-height-is-cover invariant, and it is exactly what made real trench raids so costly to hold. A physics quirk becomes the mode's central tactical dilemma: raid or hold?

2 · Men arrive, they do not appear

  • Buying = despatching a column. A purchase spawns the crew at your rear edge in single file. They path to the nearest communication trench mouth, file along the comm trench polyline (duckboard walking, already-animated gait), emerge at the assigned post, and formUp. ETA shown on the ghost at purchase time (~35–60 s by distance).
  • In-transit men are real. They can be shelled on the march — columns caught in a barrage scatter to shell holes (reuse takecover) and resume. Losing half a platoon on the way up is a story, and a reason to buy before you're desperate.
  • Implementation: a marchingIn unit state with a waypoint list built from commLines + a short flow-field leg to the post. No new pathfinder — the FlowField class is faction-agnostic and takes any target set.
  • The build/assault phase machine dissolves. This mode is continuous real time (with an opening 60 s stand-to ceasefire to set up). Reinforcement travel time is the pacing mechanism that build timers used to provide.

3 · Economy — divisional supply

  • Both sides start with the same requisition pool (~£450) and receive a steady drip (~£12 per 10 s), so matches escalate rather than stall.
  • Holding the sunken lane raises your drip by ~50%; holding captured enemy front sections raises it further. Aggression compounds.
  • Existing roster and prices carry over unchanged (UNIT_DEFS, config.ts:111); the German commander buys from a mirrored roster (same stats, German meshes) in v1 — asymmetric national rosters are a post-ship flavour pass.
  • Casualties drain a per-side battalion strength pool (the two-sided descendant of the breach meter). It is a loss condition and the anti-turtle clock.

4 · Sections, groups, and the whistle

  • Select a stretch of line by dragging along your trench (sections are already discrete data — buildSections, trench.ts:10), or stage a named assault group at the ladders. Selected sections glow with the existing brass-ribbon treatment.
  • Orders, per selection (modelled on the issueOrder pattern, game.ts:1126):
    • Over the top — whistle, ladders, and the selection's infantry form squads and advance on a chosen enemy section under the generalised squad AI: flow-field advance, bounding overwatch, wire cutting, melee, capture. This is the enemy's existing brain (enemies.ts) running southward-to-northward for our side.
    • Covering fire — remaining sections concentrate on the assault frontage (target-priority override).
    • Smoke / box barrage — existing barrage systems aimed to screen or isolate the objective.
    • Recall — break off; squads fall back to their home fire steps (the existing fallback behaviour, aimed home instead of at the support line).
    • Consolidate — engineers reverse a captured section's fire step and repair its parapet.
  • Win conditions: hold over half the enemy's front-line sections for 60 continuous seconds, or break the enemy's battalion strength. Timed variant for multiplayer lobbies: most sections held at the whistle.
  • Embodiment carries over: possess any soldier — including one marching up the comm trench, or one going over the top — via the existing FPS seam. In singleplayer this works from the first playable build.

5 · The AI commander

  • Reframe the adaptive wave director (planWave's budget/template/counter logic, waves.ts:37) as a command emitter: it holds requisition, watches the same fog-free state, and issues BuyUnit/OrderAssault commands through the same queue as a human.
  • Personas set difficulty and character: Methodical (fortifies, probes, counter-buys), Stosstrupp (hoards for big pushes behind box barrages), Opportunist (attacks whichever of your sections is thinnest — it already reads your loadout to counter-buy).
  • Because it speaks the command protocol, the AI can also take over a disconnected player mid-match — an AI commander and a netcode fallback in one.

Part IVMultiplayer — lockstep on the existing determinism

Architecture: deterministic lockstep, relay server, no server-side sim

Why lockstep: commander input is low-rate (a few commands a minute, not twitch), the sim is already fixed-step and seeded, and state is far too rich to snapshot-stream at 30 Hz. Lockstep sends only commands; both clients compute identical battles. Bandwidth: bytes per second.
  • Scheduling: a command issued at tick T executes at T+6 (~200 ms) on both clients. Imperceptible for commander orders; the local HUD acknowledges instantly (whistle sound, ribbon flash) while the sim catches up.
  • Transport v1: a ~150-line WebSocket relay (Node, fly.io/Render-class hosting) — rooms via 4-letter codes, relays command packets and tick acks, keeps the command log for late-join/rejoin. No game logic on the server at all. WebRTC DataChannel is a v2 latency upgrade that slots behind the same interface.
  • Desync defence: every 30 ticks, hash the hot state (positions, hp, req, section ownership → 32-bit rolling hash) and exchange. On mismatch: pause, one client uploads a full snapshot (the v2 save format, extended to full SimState), both resume. Desyncs become a logged, recoverable event, not a mystery.
  • Rejoin/replay: seed + command log is the match. Rejoin = fast-forward replay; spectating and post-match replays use the same path.
  • FPS embodiment in multiplayer ships in a second pass: possessed movement/fire become tick-stamped intent commands (aim vector + trigger; spread already comes from seeded RNG in fireSmallArms), with local visual prediction. Until then, MP is commander-view; SP has full embodiment from the start.

Part VIRisks & open questions

Risks

  • M3 is the heavy lift. Generalising the enemy brain to both factions touches the most files. Mitigation: option (b) — graft the Enemy behaviour struct onto player soldiers rather than unifying the pools; the ledger keeps the diff enumerable.
  • Real-time economy balance is new territory (the current game balances via discrete waves). Mitigation: M4's AI-vs-AI soak harness doubles as a balance lab — sweep drip rates headless overnight.
  • Perf with two armies + columns: instancing holds (14 draw calls), but per-frame CPU matrix writes double and corpse counts grow. Watch via ?fpslab probes; cap corpses per side.
  • Cross-engine desync is possible, detectable, and recoverable (hash → snapshot resync); the math shim is the permanent fix if it ever fires in practice.

Open questions for the CO

  • Fog of war? V1 assumes full visibility (both trenches in view, like the current game). A light fog — enemy rear hidden, front line visible — would make raids and the sunken lane observation post matter more. Cheap to add post-M3.
  • Match length target? Drip rates and strength pools tune to it — 15 minutes feels right for MP; SP could run longer.
  • Asymmetric rosters? V1 mirrors the British roster for the Germans. A national-flavour pass (MG08 nests, Stosstruppen) is pure config once M3 lands.
  • Relay hosting: happy to stand up the ~150-line relay on fly.io when M5 starts — zero game logic server-side, negligible cost.

What lands for free along the way

  • Replays of any match (seed + command log) — including the existing defence campaign once it routes through the command spine.
  • Headless match harness — AI-vs-AI soak tests join the repo's soak-test pattern, now for full battles.
  • March-in for the classic mode — reinforcements walking up the comm trench would suit the defence campaign too, if we want it.
  • Disconnect-proof MP — the AI commander is the fallback for any absent human, forever.
