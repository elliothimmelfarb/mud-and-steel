M6 — the last debts paid

Embodiment joins the command spine

  • possess / release / fpspose / fpsfire / fpstool — stepping into a man's boots, walking him, firing his rifle, dressing a mate's wounds: all tick-stamped commands now. Muzzle flash, report and recoil stay on the click-frame; the bullet spawns at the next boundary (≤33 ms). An embodied battle run through twin sims and a cold replay lands on one identical hash.
  • The War Diary: every finished Big Push battle records itself — seed, commander, and the full envelope log, which is the battle. A title-screen button (or ?replay) refights it in the real renderer: speed keys work, the leash is off, and your orders are politely refused. History does not take amendments.
  • Rejoin, for real: drop mid-match and the survivor quietly holds the room open (the AI minds your trench); come back with the same code and the hello's tick tells your client to fetch the war diary and fast-forward — the same rebuild the desync-recovery probe proved bit-exact.
  • Small mercies: the German commander's camera leash now mirrors properly, verdicts read from the seat you actually occupy, a heartbeat catches hard-killed tabs, and the casualty roster is written by the sim itself — the memorial no longer depends on who was watching.
Adversarial verification

Two more skeptics, four more scalps

The embodiment refactor got its own panel. The sealed findings — a release command eaten by the game-over gate (leaving a zombie possession haunting endless mode), embodiment reachable inside a replay (a ghost body firing blanks over recorded history), the lab's invincibility pin unguarded in net matches, and a medic who could dress his own wounds — were all fixed before merge. The full suite then re-ran green: classic 20-wave twin+replay tick-identical to the M0 gate, bigpush twin clean, kill-and-rejoin lockstep at zero desyncs.

Held back deliberately: MP embodiment stays off. It is determinism-safe now, but fpsfire/fpspose trust the sending client — a cheat surface that wants sim-side rate and position gating, and the six-tick input delay wants a human feel playtest, before a stranger gets a rifle (#55).
