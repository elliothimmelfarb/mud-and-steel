Second Army · Flanders · Field Despatch

The Line Rebuilt

A realism overhaul of the trench system, end to end — 18th July, 1916 (2026)
Secret

The trenches had been through several rounds of polish and still read as grassy gullies with beads on top. This round rebuilt them against the photographic record: the crenellated trace from the air, the bare trodden earth around every dig, sandbags that sag under their own weight, revetment that lies against the wall it holds, wire in front of every parapet — and a German line on the horizon where until now there was only empty grass.

The Trace · terrain.ts

Crenellation — the aerial signature

The front and support lines were smooth triangle-wave zigzags; from the commander's view they read as wavy ditches. Every aerial photograph of a British fire trench shows the same thing instead: straight fire bays broken by square traverse teeth, so no shell burst or enfilading burst can sweep more than one bay.

BeforeA ~14 m sawtooth; slots and parapet ran continuously around every corner.
AfterPer 14 m period: a ~9.5 m bay forward, a square jog back, a 4.5 m link behind the tooth, a jog forward. Fighting sections, fire-step benches and slots live on the bays only; traverses stay bare earth.

The carve is now two-pass: every line's floor first, then the parapet/parados banks, with a guard that skips any cell another corridor has already cut (or a bench has claimed). Banks can no longer wall off a crossing corridor — which is also what used to spill spoil into the zigzag corners. Communication trenches now zigzag properly every ~8.5 m instead of one long dogleg.

The Ground · terrain.ts + terrainMesh + clutter

Bare earth where men dig and walk

The single biggest lie in the old picture: grass ran unbroken down the walls, across the floor and over both banks. Trenchworks kill turf for metres around — digging, spoil, and ten thousand boots.

A ragged trodden-earth halo now runs along every line — walls, floor, parapet and parados — painted into churnVis only, the render-side churn channel, so the shader dresses the works in mud without adding a single point of movement cost (gameplay churn stays shell-driven). Grass tufts read the same channel and no longer sprout from the parapet crest.

Elliot's Eye · groundcover.ts

Sandbags that have settled

“Too perky and tall. They should be flatter and feel like they are sagging on top of each other.” — quite right. The bags were scaled spheres perched in a bead-string.

  • Every bag reshaped: wider and 30% lower, crown flattened and faintly saddled, skirt bulged outward — a sack that has taken the weight of the course above.
  • Courses nest: three rows now interlock (each row's base sits below the crown of the last) and upper bags roll a few degrees into the valleys beneath them.
  • The wall nests into the crest (dropped 7 cm into the bank) instead of perching on it; the dugout's flanking stacks compressed to match the new bag height.
Elliot's Eye · scenery.ts

Revetment that touches the wall

“The wood slats aren't against the walls — there are gaps between the slats and the wall which is very jarring.” The panels stood plumb vertical against a wall that slopes; daylight showed behind every board.

  • Raked to the measured slope: each panel samples the wall it faces and leans back to lie on the earth, base pushed into the wall foot.
  • Corner-guarded: every piece probes both of its ends before placing — if a crossing corridor cut the wall away (the 90° turns), the piece skips itself rather than jut into the path.
  • Fire-step face lowered from a 1.5 m centreline fence to a 1.0 m boarded lower riser under an earth lip — the two-tier step of the manuals, and no more planks poking over the skyline from behind.
The Enemy · terrain.ts + scenery.ts + waves.ts

A German line on the horizon

No-man's land used to fade into empty pasture — no man ever held a front opposite nobody. A south-facing German fire trench (same crenellated trace, mirrored banks) now stands at the north edge behind the enemy spawn, sandbagged along its parapet, with a scenery wire belt in front of it, cut by the assault lanes the field-grey columns pour through. Enemy vehicles now spawn abreast instead of in a deep column, so nothing materialises inside the German works.

The Wire · game.ts

No line ever stood behind bare grass

Fresh runs now begin behind two staggered rows of worn wire (reduced hp, high wear — rusty and sagging, not a free fortress), with sally-port gaps on the communication-trench axes and a couple of random blast gaps. The gaps are the funnels; the wire is the reason wave one doesn't walk in shoulder to shoulder. Resumed saves restore exactly the wire they had.

Furniture · groundcover.ts + scenery.ts

Ladders, iron, periscopes, honest duckboards

  • Scaling ladders lean mid-bay against the enemy wall, heads over the parapet — placed only where the bench truly exists.
  • Corrugated-iron sheets (sine-rippled, rust-bloomed, one dog-eared corner) lean on the parados here and there as scrounged revetment.
  • Trench periscopes peek over the parapet on a handful of bays.
  • Duckboards finally behave: they skip benches, junction steps and any ground steep enough that a board would climb a wall — the ladder-up-the-wall artefact is gone.

Verification

  • Gallery presets screenshotted: overview (crenellated line + German works + bare-earth band), front-trench close (settled bags, tucked revetment, clean skyline), no-man's-land wet (German horizon holds).
  • FPS lab: fire-step view over the parapet — wire pickets and the far line read; in-trench view — bags sag along the crest, planks lie on the walls.
  • Corner probe script re-ran the exact placement math against the live terrain: zero wall pieces protrude above grade.
  • Live wave smoke test (sim pumped headless): 20 Germans spawned clear of their own works, crossed the lanes and the British belt, entered the bays, captured sections; wire, capture and flow all behave. tsc --noEmit clean.
