Over The Top

Why the push never arrived, and what it takes to get it there · 27 July 1916

A push sent over the top could not cross no-man's-land. It went to ground under fire and stayed there for the rest of the match — while the renderer animated every man in it at a full-tilt run. Three separate faults compounded into one wall. This is what they were, and how the assault was rebuilt around a single rule: nothing may ever stop a man completely.

Cause the first

Flat psychological costs, billed thirty times a second

Every point of damage in the game runs through one function, and it charged a flat +0.15 suppression and −0.08 morale per call — regardless of how badly the man was hurt.

That is fine for a rifle round. It is catastrophic for the continuous sources — wire, gas, flame — which call it every tick for a fraction of a point of damage each. Standing in an enemy belt therefore cost +4.5 suppression and −2.4 morale per second.

  • A man who so much as brushed the wire was pinned permanently.
  • His whole push broke on the spot — measured, with zero casualties taken.
  • Enemy wire was not an obstacle. It was an impassable wall.

Both costs now scale with the hurt, which leaves a bullet exactly where it was:

target.morale      -= Math.min(COMBAT.moraleHitPenalty, dmg * 0.006)
target.suppression += Math.min(0.15,                   dmg * 0.015)
Cause the second

The pin was a terminal state

if (c.suppression > COMBAT.suppressPin) { c.stance = 'prone'; continue }

One line, and everything downstream of it unreachable. A pinned man could not crawl, could not return fire, and could not cut the wire he was stuck in — because the cutting code sat below the pin check.

The telling part: the enemy's own attack brain, which this was copied from, already solves all of this. It has a take-cover state that resumes at suppression < 0.22, a dedicated wire-cutting state, and suppression that only ever scales speed to 0.45× — never zero. The player's copy dropped exactly the three mechanisms that make it work.

Cause the third

Nerve was a latch, not a state

Suppression drained at 0.09/s. A single MG 08 pours on roughly 1.0/s — two-thirds from the burst, the rest from near misses striking the dirt around the target. An eleven-to-one ratio.

Anything a machine gun looked at stayed pinned until the gun died, however long that took. There was no counterplay, because putting fire on their trench did nothing your men could feel.

At 0.22/s a man shakes it off in about a second of quiet. That single number closes the loop the mode was missing: suppressing their garrison now visibly unpins your assault.

The rebuild

One rule, held everywhere

No state in an assault may reduce a man's speed to zero. An attack that can stall forever is an order the player can never learn to use; one that is merely expensive is a decision.

  • Pinned means crawling. Flat on his face at 1.25 m/s, weaving crater to crater, answering the fire lying down. Wire is resolved first and unconditionally, so the man in the belt is the man cutting it — engineers fastest.
  • Cover-seeking with no memory. Five headings sampled inside a 110° fan, deepest ground wins, biased back toward the objective. Nothing stored, so no cover target goes stale when the next shell digs a better hole two seconds later — and it stays deterministic.
  • Pushes break and run home. Lose 60% of the men or their nerve and the survivors go back over their own parapet. Legible, diagnosable failure instead of an invisible stall.
  • The bound only starts in range. Fire-and-movement inside 70 m of the objective, where the static element's rifles can actually reach the parapet they are keeping down — and it lies prone, not crouched half-upright in the open.
  • Silence is gone. Every refusal says why. The order announces the objective and the number away. A ring marks each push still out.
The other half of the assault

The barrage that was locked out of its own game mode

Creeping Barrage Doctrine is a tier-2 upgrade, gated on reaching wave 6. The Big Push has no wave counter — it is one continuous battle that sits at wave 1 forever. The whole tier-2 and tier-3 shelf was quietly unreachable in the mode, the barrage among it.

So the assault had no preparation tool at all. Now:

  • Buyable in the Big Push — there, requisition is the gate, the same drip that buys men.
  • Aimed where the commander is looking, and walking a 70 m frontage rather than smearing 132 shells uniformly across the whole 210 m map, which suppressed nobody and cut nothing.
  • An assault leans on it instead of sprinting into its own shells — a fresh section runs at 3.6 m/s and the curtain walks at 2.3, so without a lag rule the men overtake their own artillery inside half a minute.
  • It steadies the men following it. The shells still kill — that cost stays — but they no longer rout the attack they were sent to carry.
Your own wire

The hedge in front of your parapet

Two staggered rows of worn British wire sit at z ≈ 65–70, ten to fourteen metres out from the fire trench, with nine-metre sally ports on the communication-trench axes and a couple of seeded blast gaps. It is real simulation wire, not scenery, and it does its job against German counterattacks.

But your own assault skipped it entirely — British troops ghosted through the British belt as though it were painted on. It now costs tempo (0.62×) without blood or cutting: you know your lanes and the boards go down before the whistle, but a belt you walk through as if it were not there is a belt that should not be drawn.

Classic mode re-checked end to end — five waves held, breach untouched at 100. Confirmed in the running game too: an eighteen-man push crosses the whole of no-man's-land and fights at the German parapet through a lane cut in their wire.

And the animation stopped lying

moveAmount was a boolean: anyone in an assault group rendered at a full run whether he was sprinting, on overwatch, or lying pinned. That is the "running for a minute and going nowhere" that was reported. It is now measured from ground actually covered, along with the stride clock. Sampled live mid-assault, the gait values across one group spanned 0 (holding), 0.27 (crawling) and 1 (running).

Left open

One soft spot, honestly

Barrage timing wants real play. At 2.3 m/s the curtain lifts off the objective before the last men arrive. Slowing it to match the infantry measured worse, because the men then pace themselves to it and spend twice as long in the open. The shipped settings are the ones that never produced a catastrophic result in probing — but that is a feel question two hand-built seeds cannot settle.
