What landed

Host a match · Join with code · or two tabs on one machine

  • The lobby lives on the Big Push title panel: Host a Match opens a room (4-letter code, 15-minute TTL) through /api/rooms on Vercel; Join with Code answers it; the WebRTC DataChannel opens and the signaling server leaves the conversation for good. STUN only, v1.
  • The German seat is playable: his own requisition purse in the top strip, hotkeys 1–4 despatching rifle squads, MG teams, Stosstruppen and pioneers to the line, and the same section-click grammar mirrored — select your bays, click an enemy one, over the top. The British bottom bar is his no longer.
  • Time belongs to both commanders: speed locked to 1, no pause, menus and modals do not stop the battle. If the other side's frames stop coming, a toast says so within three seconds instead of leaving a silent freeze.
  • Leaving is handled like a soldier: quit, restart, or close the tab and a bye crosses first — the survivor's machine seats the AI at the abandoned German desk, or claims the walkover if Britain's commander deserted.
The machinery

Sealed frames: a proof, not a heuristic

After every stepped tick a peer sends frame T. Because the channel is ordered and every order is stamped tick+6 before it is sent, holding frame T proves you hold every peer envelope for ticks ≤ T+6 — so the gate next ≤ peerFrame + 6 makes late input structurally impossible rather than merely unlikely. Hashes cross every 30 ticks; a mismatch triggers the one recovery path there is: rebuild from the creator's seed + envelope log, the same replay contract the probes have been enforcing since M0. Rejoin after a dropped connection is that same rebuild with a new transport.

Adversarial verification

Three skeptics, briefed to refute it

Per standing orders for determinism-touching work, three independent reviewers attacked the merged diff along different bearings — protocol interleavings, hidden state and purity, integration seams. The core held every attack: the one-tick margin of the sealed-frame gate, the rebuild arithmetic, state-adoption coverage, and the AI-takeover logging contract were each explicitly assaulted and survived. What they refuted was the failure-of-the-failure-path layer — fourteen confirmed findings, all fixed the same evening: a gate deadlock when a peer died mid-desync, orders half-applied during a rebuild, quits that never said goodbye, a signaling mailbox that discarded messages when one throw landed mid-batch, ICE candidates lost to an ordering race, a third local tab corrupting a two-tab room.

Two findings were deferred with eyes open: cross-engine float determinism (transcendentals differ between browser engines in the last bits — matches are effectively same-engine until sim math goes software) and production rejoin wiring (the machinery is probe-proven; the lobby flow to re-signal a dropped match is M6).
